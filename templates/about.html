About

Noted is a web-based internet application for the creation and sharing of lecture notes. It has been developed by Korey Hawsey as a part of his Final Year Computer Science module at the University of Westminster. For guidance on the included templates, please refer to the visuals below or to Oxford Learning's guide. For any further guidance or help, please contact me here.

Cornell Method
The Cornell method is great for creating organized notes with summaries that make them easy to review and use for studying. The note is divided into three sections: cues, notes, and summary. Firstly, the notes section is located in the top-right and is where the notes will be written during the lecture. Next is the cues section which is located in the top-left and is where main ideas and topics will be added after the lecture. Lastly, the summary section is located at the bottom and is where the key points and information will be summarised after the lecture.

Sentence Method
The Sentence method is an easy and efficient method for capturing a lot of information very quickly. Each new point is a new line, so that each individual point can be easily distinguished. Additional headings can also be added to separate by different topics.

Outlining Method
The Outlining method is best for creating notes with topics that contain a lot of detail. Its nested structure allows for easily visible relationships between topics and subtopics. Each main topic has its own heading and its relative subtopics and each subtopic has its own list of key points.

Charting Method
The Charting method separates information into distinct categories that each occupies their own column. This method is best suited for topics that cover a lot of facts or realtionships between topics. Each column has its distinct subheading and then a list of relevant details.

Mapping Method (update pic)
The Mapping method is the most visual method and is best for representing relationships between topics. Start with the main topic and then branch out into each of its subtopics, then put the key details and lastly the trail of interconnected points.
